\section{Introduction}

Program transformations often treat logging, representation, inspection, caching, and repeated access as behavior-preserving. That treatment is valid when the accessed operation is observational in the semantic sense. It can fail when the operation updates latent state that a later read exposes. We call this pattern access-induced semantic divergence. OSDS adds structure beyond detecting unequal executions: it models the latent state changed by observation-shaped accesses, defines the access-order witness to compare, and specifies neutralizing interventions for attribution.

The phenomenon is related to observer effects and probe effects, but the paper studies a specific sequential mechanism: an observation-shaped access mutates latent access state, and a later program result changes. This matters for generated code because requested edits are often local and plausible. A model may add instrumentation, memoize a representation, or simplify repeated access while preserving ordinary tests that do not vary observation order.

We study this mechanism through three evidence levels. First, phenomenon breadth: the real-code oracle confirms 20 divergences across 12 unmodified Python packages, with 9 caller-level effects and 19/19 applicable real-code mechanism controls eliminating divergence. Second, generated-code failures: the frozen primary benchmark contains five verified model-task OSDS failures over two underlying package witnesses, pytest and PyYAML, and all five were silent under the benchmark ordinary checks. Third, prospective limitation: a prospectively frozen expansion built from previously confirmed unused witnesses produced 42 model outputs, of which 23 were task-compliant transformations, 19 returned unchanged code, 23/23 compliant transformations were OSDS-preserving, and 0/42 produced verified OSDS divergence.

The empirical claim is bounded. We do not estimate ecosystem prevalence, and the three tested Codex task-model configurations are not independent model providers. The strongest generated-code evidence is the causal chain in the primary benchmark: five generated transformations passed ordinary checks, failed OSDS-aware verification, reproduced under exact replay, and disappeared under mechanism-neutralizing controls while the generated candidate stayed fixed. These are five model-task outcomes over two underlying package witnesses, not a frequency estimate.

\textbf{Contributions.} We make four contributions. First, we give an OSDS mechanism model and structural properties for access-state-carrying sequential systems. Second, we give a real-code witness methodology and report 20 confirmed divergences across 12 packages, including 9 caller-level branch effects. Third, we evaluate frozen generated transformations and identify five silent model-task OSDS failures over two package witnesses. Fourth, we combine mechanism-neutralizing causal attribution with a prospectively frozen null expansion that separates task compliance from semantic preservation.
